The Framework

Kindergartner · Ages 5–10

Five years from curious to exceptional

For a kindergartner, the mission is clear: build a child who loves learning so deeply that academic excellence becomes inevitable. The first two years are entirely play-based. Years 3–5 introduce structured enrichment, competition, and creative output.

Grade 3 Student · Ages 8–13

From grade-level to exceptional in five years

A Grade 3 student can begin serious academic acceleration immediately. The goal is to be doing Grade 10-level work by the end of Year 5 — placing them perfectly for IB/AP enrollment, which is Harvard's true pipeline.

For parents

Your 1-hour daily playbook

You are the coach, not the teacher. Here's exactly how to spend your one hour in a way that multiplies everything else.

🌅
Morning (15 min)
Over breakfast: ask one big question ("What makes something fair?"), share a word of the day, pose a math puzzle. Activate their brain before school. This 15 minutes sets the intellectual tone for the entire day.
📖
After-school check-in (10 min)
Ask: "What's one thing you were curious about today?" and "What's one thing that was hard?" Never just "how was school?" Document wins in a shared achievement log. Celebrate effort over outcome, always.
🍽️
Dinner conversation (20 min)
This is your highest-leverage time. Debate a current event, discuss a book chapter, share discoveries. Harvard students are exceptional conversationalists — it starts at the dinner table, years before applications.
🌙
Bedtime ritual (15 min)
Read together. Ask 3 gratitude items. For Grade 3: review their achievement log and set one goal for tomorrow. Sleep is critical for learning consolidation — protect it fiercely. Target 9–10 hours.
📊
Weekly review (Sunday, 15 min)
What milestones moved? What needs more focus? Update their "brag folder" with new achievements. Preview the coming week together. Make them feel like partners in their own development — not passengers.
🚨
What to avoid
Never criticize effort. Never compare siblings. Never let screen entertainment replace reading time. Never skip the bedtime routine. Never prioritize grades over curiosity. Burnout is the only way this plan fails.
