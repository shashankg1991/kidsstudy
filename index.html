Time allocation

Who does what — and when

Three roles, three time blocks. Every session below is colour-coded so each person always knows exactly what they are doing and why.

🧑‍👧
You — the parent coach
1 hour · split across the day
Morning (15 min): Brain warm-up at breakfast — one question, one puzzle, one word of the day.

Dinner (20 min): Discussion, debate, curiosity conversation — this is where real intellectual confidence is built.

Bedtime (15 min): Read aloud together, 3 gratitudes, set tomorrow's goal.

Sunday (10 min): Weekly review, achievement log update, preview of coming week.
📐
Tutor
1 hour · after school · 4:00–5:00 PM
Math (25 min): One level above grade — explicit teaching, worked examples, guided practice, error analysis.

Writing (20 min): Current writing project — teach one technique, student practises it immediately.

Science / Enrichment (15 min): Introduce the week's experiment or project. Assign the self-directed steps.

Tutor receives weekly prep notes from this plan — no guesswork.
🎒
Kids — self-directed
4–5 hrs/day · independently
School day: Be the most engaged student. Ask one great question per class. Raise your hand.

After tutor (5:00–6:00): Independent reading (30 min) + instrument practice (20 min) + free creative time (10 min).

Evening: French app (15 min), science project step (10 min), reading log entry (5 min).

Every self-directed task has exact instructions — no parent needed to explain it.
Kindergartner · Ages 5–10

Five years to fall in love with learning

Play-based in Years 1–2. Structured enrichment from Year 3. Competitions and creative output in Years 4–5. The goal is a 10-year-old who reads at Grade 7 level and thinks like a scientist.

Grade 3 Student · Ages 8–13

From grade-level to exceptional in five years

Immediate acceleration — 1–2 grade levels above in every subject from week one. By Year 5 this student is doing Grade 10 work, perfectly positioned for IB or AP and a strong university trajectory.

For you

Your exact 1-hour daily script

You are not the teacher. You are the coach, the questioner, the celebrator. Here is precisely how to spend your hour.

🌅
7:30 AM · Morning spark (15 min)
Over breakfast, pick one: (1) "Would you rather" ethical dilemma. (2) Math puzzle on paper. (3) Share a strange fact and ask why. (4) Word of the day — look up etymology together. The goal is to send them to school with their brain already switched on.
🍽️
6:30 PM · Dinner discussion (20 min)
Never ask "how was school?" Ask instead: "What is one thing that confused you today?" or "What question did you ask a teacher?" or "Tell me something that surprised you." Then debate it. Strong candidates everywhere are extraordinary conversationalists — it starts here.
🌙
7:45 PM · Bedtime ritual (15 min)
Read aloud a book one level above what they read alone. Stop and ask one prediction question. After the chapter: three gratitudes (you say one too). Then one goal for tomorrow — they say it, you write it on a sticky note on their door. Done by 8 PM.
📊
Sunday · Weekly review (10 min)
Sit with each child separately for 5 minutes. Ask: "What are you most proud of this week?" Update the achievement log together. Add any new milestone completed. Preview the coming week. Set one goal. That is your entire Sunday involvement.
📋
Tutor coordination (5 min/week)
On Sunday, send a 3-line message to the tutor: (1) what milestone we are working toward, (2) what the child found hard this week, (3) what to focus on Monday. Use the week plan from this site. The tutor does not need to plan — they execute the plan.
🚫
What not to do
Never criticise effort. Never compare the two children. Never say "you are so smart" — say "you worked hard on that." Never skip bedtime reading. Never check their homework for errors — let the tutor do that. Your job is to make learning feel like the most natural thing in the world.
